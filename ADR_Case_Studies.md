Suspected Adverse Drug Reaction to Intravenous Immunoglobulin (IVIG)

1. Patient Information

- Patient initials: EA
- Age: 6 years
- Sex: Male
- Body weight: 17 kg
- Diagnosis: Kawasaki disease

2. Clinical Presentation

A 6-year-old male child, identified as EA, weighing 17 kg, was admitted with a history of fever, swollen neck, abdominal pain, joint pain and reddened tongue. Following clinical assessment, the patient was diagnosed with Kawasaki disease.

3. Laboratory Investigations

The following laboratory results were recorded:

Laboratory Parameter| Result
Haemoglobin (Hb)| 10.0 g/dL
Total Count (TC)| 10,900 cells/mm³
Platelet count| 2.87 lakhs/mm³
Packed Cell Volume (PCV)| 33.5%
Mean Corpuscular Volume (MCV)| 78.8 fL

4. Suspected Medicine Details

- Medicine name: Intravenous Immunoglobulin (IVIG)
- Manufacturer: Reliance Life Sciences
- Batch number: ID05G21020
- Manufacturing date: September 2022
- Expiry date: August 2025
- Reported dose: 2 g/kg/day
- Route of administration: Intravenous
- Frequency: Once daily (OD)
- Date treatment commenced: 23 June 2023

5. Description of the Adverse Drug Reaction

Treatment with intravenous immunoglobulin was initiated on 23 June 2023.

Following administration of the medicine on the same day, the patient developed generalized itching (pruritus) and skin rashes.

The suspected medicine was withdrawn on 24 June 2023. The reaction was managed with intravenous chlorpheniramine and intravenous dexamethasone. The patient recovered from the reaction on 24 June 2023.

However, the suspected medicine was administered again on 27 June 2023. Following re-administration, generalized itching recurred on the same day.

The medicine was subsequently discontinued, and appropriate treatment was administered.

6. ADR Identification and Assessment

Suspected adverse drug reaction: Generalized pruritus and skin rash.

Suspected medicine: Intravenous Immunoglobulin (IVIG).

Time relationship: The reaction occurred on the same day as the initial administration of IVIG.

Dechallenge: The suspected medicine was withdrawn, and the patient recovered following treatment with intravenous chlorpheniramine and dexamethasone.

Rechallenge: Following re-administration of IVIG on 27 June 2023, generalized itching recurred on the same day.

Assessment: The temporal relationship between IVIG administration and the onset of symptoms suggests a possible association. Furthermore, the recurrence of itching following re-exposure provides strong evidence supporting a relationship between the suspected medicine and the reported reaction.

The findings are suggestive of a hypersensitivity-type reaction. However, further clinical assessment would be required to establish the exact immunological mechanism.

7. Suspected ADR Classification

- Type of reaction: Suspected hypersensitivity-type reaction.
- Clinical manifestations: Generalized itching and skin rash.
- Seriousness: Requires assessment using the patient's clinical records and applicable pharmacovigilance criteria.
- Outcome: Recovered from the initial reaction; recurrence occurred following re-exposure.
- Causality: The positive rechallenge strongly supports a probable association with IVIG, subject to complete clinical assessment.

8. Management of the Reaction

The suspected medicine was withdrawn following the initial reaction. The patient received intravenous chlorpheniramine and intravenous dexamethasone, after which recovery was recorded.

Following recurrence of itching after re-exposure, the medicine was stopped and appropriate treatment was administered.

9. Conclusion

This case describes a suspected adverse drug reaction associated with intravenous immunoglobulin administration in a 6-year-old male patient diagnosed with Kawasaki disease.

The development of generalized itching and skin rash following the initial administration, improvement after withdrawal and recurrence of itching following re-administration suggest a strong temporal association between IVIG exposure and the reported reaction.

The positive rechallenge is particularly important in assessing the likelihood of a drug-related reaction. This case demonstrates the importance of monitoring patients during medicine administration, documenting suspected adverse reactions and reporting them to the appropriate pharmacovigilance authorities.
